Cocoa is not just a market.

 

It is a chain of people, soil and decisions.

 

Dear Cacao Latitude team,

I hope this email finds you well. I'll begin the conventional way.

I am currently completing a Master's degree in Investment & Financial Risk Management in Paris, learning markets: price Equity, Fixed Income, derivatives, commodities, financial analysis, hedging...

I have worked in coffee export in Vietnam, in agricultural trading environments, and in maritime logistics within vegetable oils. I understand contracts, risk exposure, and what demurrage does to P&L.

On paper, I am a junior profile interested in commodities.

But that is only part of the story.

 

That was the formal version.

The deeper truth is that I am driven by the product itself.

Cocoa. Coffee. Tea.

I grew up in Côte d'Ivoire and lived there for ten years. I return regularly. In January, I left Abidjan before sunrise to visit producers near M'Batto. I met cooperative members showing me their storage rooms filled to capacity. I spent time in a village nursery with a man trained in Israel and Brazil, now cultivating cocoa seedlings in his hometown.

Meeting with Agrofangan, near M'Batto, Côte d'Ivoire

Those encounters matter to me more than any supply curve ever could.

I am the kind of person who checks the price per kilo of chocolate bars in supermarkets to reverse-engineer value distribution. I read labels searching for origin clues. I once scanned a QR code behind a Kit Kat bar packaging simply out of curiosity.

The fascination is not theoretical. It is tangible.

Cocoa producer members from the village of Angamaki

In Vietnam, I moved to Buon Ma Thuot for three months to work with a coffee exporter. Daily cuppings, plantation visits, warehouse inspections. By coincidence, I visited ACOM's warehouse in Lam Dong and shared dinner with their team. A small world, this name certainly rings a bell for you.

Visiting the ACOM warehouse, Lam Dong province, Vietnam

What interests me is not only trading cocoa, but understanding it across the entire chain: post-harvest processes, grading, quality control, logistics, sales structures, risk management.

Most finance graduates do not get excited about tanker chartering strategies, statement-of-fact analysis to optimize demurrage exposure, or spending hours alongside SGS inspectors sampling bags prior to shipment.

I do.

Logistics, trade mechanics, sourcing, product specificity, I absorb them quickly because I genuinely care about them. Agronomy is an area I still need to deepen, and I am conscious of it. It is probably the next layer I want to truly understand.

Sharing dinner with the ECOM team in Vietnam

 

I am not looking for a title.

Trader.
Merchant.
Quality controller.
Supply chain officer.

What I am looking for is an environment where the product is central, where origin understanding meets commercial discipline.

Cacao Latitude stands precisely at that intersection: proximity to origins, structured market positioning, and long-term development vision.

The challenging reality of Ivorian cocoa

I will be available from July 2026, upon completion of my Master's.

If this profile resonates, I would be glad to speak with you.

 

And in the meantime, should your office ever need quality roasted coffee beans, my small project SONECA Roast might finance my next origin trip to... where? I don't know for the moment.

Maybe Sri Lanka to meet the people behind tea auctions.
Maybe Panama or Costa Rica to understand the economics behind €300/kg coffees.
Maybe back to Vietnam to look at emerging cacao production in the central highlands.
